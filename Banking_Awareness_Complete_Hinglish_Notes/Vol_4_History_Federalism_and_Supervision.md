## 🏛️ Module 21: History of Banking in India & Bank Nationalization

### 1. Phase 1: Pre-Independence Period (1770 to 1947) — *[PDF Pages 128–131]*
* **1770:** Bharat ka sabse pehla bank **Bank of Hindustan** Calcutta me establish hua (by Alexander and Co.). Ceased operations in 1832.
* **1786:** General Bank of India establish hua (liquidated in 1791).
* **The Three Presidency Banks:**
  1. **Bank of Bengal (1806)** — Shuru me Bank of Calcutta (1806), renamed Bank of Bengal in 1809.
  2. **Bank of Bombay (1840)**
  3. **Bank of Madras (1843)**
* **Imperial Bank of India (1921):**
  * **27 January 1921** ko teeno Presidency Banks ko merge karke **Imperial Bank of India** banaya gaya.
  * Imperial Bank commercial banking ke sath-sath **1935 me RBI banne tak Quasi-Central Bank** (Government banker aur clearing house) ke roop me bhi kaam karta tha!
* **Swadeshi Movement (1905–1906) Ka Impetus [PDF Page 129]:**
  * Swadeshi andolan se prerit hokar Bharatiya promoters ne kai commercial banks shuru kiye:
    * **Punjab National Bank (1894/95)**
    * **Canara Bank (1906)** — Originally Canara Hindu Permanent Fund
    * **Bank of India (1906)**
    * **Indian Bank (1907)**
    * **Bank of Baroda (1908)**
    * **Central Bank of India (1911)**
    * **Bank of Mysore (1913)**

---

### 2. Phase 2: Post-Independence to Nationalization (1947 to 1969) — *[PDF Pages 131–133]*
* **Creation of State Bank of India (SBI - 1955):**
  * **All India Rural Credit Survey Committee (Gorwala Committee - 1954)** ne recommend kiya ki Imperial Bank of India ka nationalization karke ek state-partnered institution banaya jaye.
  * **1 July 1955** ko **State Bank of India Act, 1955** ke tehat Imperial Bank of India ka naam badalkar **State Bank of India (SBI)** kar diya gaya.
* **State Bank of India (Subsidiary Banks) Act, 1959:**
  * 8 princely state banks ko SBI ke subsidiaries ke roop me take over kiya gaya: State Bank of Hyderabad, State Bank of Bikaner, State Bank of Jaipur, State Bank of Saurashtra, State Bank of Indore, State Bank of Patiala, State Bank of Mysore, aur State Bank of Travancore.
* **Social Control vs Nationalization Debate [PDF Page 133]:**
  * 1960s me commercial banks private industrial houses ke control me the. Desh ka 80% bank credit sirf big corporates ko ja raha tha, jabki agriculture ko 2% se bhi kam credit milta tha.
  * Deputy PM aur Finance Minister **Morarji Desai** cautious "Social Control" ke favour me the (boards me professional control lana), jabki Prime Minister **Indira Gandhi** complete state ownership (Nationalization) chahti thin.

#### 📈 Positives vs Negatives of Nationalization [PDF Page 135]:
* **Positives:**
  * Massive Branch Expansion: Population per bank branch **65,000 (1969) se girkar 14,000 (1990) par aa gayi**!
  * Rural deposit share **6.3% se badhkar 15.5%** ho gaya.
  * Priority Sector Lending institutionalise hua (kisaano ko loans milne lage).
* **Negatives:**
  * Bureaucracy aur political interference badha; customer service deteriorate hui.
  * Populous loan melas aur loan waivers se banks ke NPAs sky-rocket kar gaye, profit margins zero ke barabar ho gaye.

### 4. Phase 3: Post-1991 Banking Reforms & Private Sector Entry — *[PDF Pages 136–137]*
* **Narasimham Committee I (1991):**
  * SLR aur CRR ko step-by-step reduce karne ki recommendation di.
  * Interest rates ko deregulate kiya.
  * **Private Sector Banks ki entry allow ki** (1993 RBI Guidelines: HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, UTI Bank/Axis Bank, IndusInd Bank, Global Trust Bank setup hue).
* **Narasimham Committee II (1998):**
  * Strong banks ke merger aur consolidation ki baat kahi.
  * Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) ko strengthen kiya aur prudential norms badhaye.
* **Subsequent Licensing Rounds [PDF Page 137]:**
  * **2003–04:** Kotak Mahindra Bank aur Yes Bank ko license mila.
  * **2014:** IDFC First Bank aur Bandhan Bank ko license mila.
* **Mega Bank Consolidation (2019–2020):**
  * Public Sector Banks ko consolidate karke **27 PSBs se ghata kar sirf 12 PSBs** kar diya gaya (e.g., PNB me OBC aur United Bank of India; Canara me Syndicate Bank; Union Bank me Andhra aur Corporation Bank; Indian Bank me Allahabad Bank).

## 📊 Module 22: 15th Finance Commission & Fiscal Federalism

### 1. Constitutional Mandate & Profile — *[PDF Pages 138–140]*
* **Constitutional Article:** **Article 280** of the Constitution of India mandate karta hai ki President har 5 saal me Finance Commission constitute karenge.
* **15th Finance Commission Chairman:** **Shri N. K. Singh** (Nand Kishore Singh).
* **Title of the Report:** *"Finance Commission in COVID Times"*.
* **Award Period:** **2021–22 to 2025–26 (5 Years)** *(plus ek interim report for 2020–21)*.
* **Core Mandate:** Central government ke net tax proceeds ko Centre aur States ke beech (Vertical Devolution) aur States ke aapas me (Horizontal Devolution) distribute karne ka formula tay karna.

---

### 2. Vertical Devolution (Centre to States Share) — *[PDF Page 138]*
* 15th Finance Commission ne States ke liye tax devolution share **41% recommend kiya**!
* **1% Ka Drop Kyon Hua?**
  * 14th Finance Commission ne **42%** share diya tha.
  * 15th FC ne ise 1% kam karke 41% isliye kiya kyunki **Jammu & Kashmir state ko bifurcate karke do nayi Union Territories (J&K aur Ladakh)** banaya gaya, jinki security aur funding ki responsibility ab Central Government ke pool se hoti hai.

---

```

+------------------------------------+-----------+-------------------------+
| Criteria                           | Weight (%)| Logic & Objective       |
+------------------------------------+-----------+-------------------------+
| **Income Distance**                | **45.0%** | Poorer states ko zyada  |
|                                    |           | share dena for equity   |

| **Demographic Performance**        | **12.5%** | Fertility rate (TFR)    |
|                                    |           | control karne ka reward |

+------------------------------------+-----------+-------------------------+

+------------------------------------+-----------+-------------------------+
```

> * **2011 Census Population:** 15th FC ne 1971 census ko completely drop karke **2011 Census** use kiya.
> * **Demographic Performance (12.5%):** Southern states ko population control karne par nuksan na ho, isliye unhe lower Total Fertility Rate (TFR) achieve karne ke liye 12.5% ka alag incentive weightage diya gaya!

## 📈 Module 23: Evolution of Benchmark Lending Rates (BPLR, Base Rate, MCLR, EBLR)

### 1. Interest Rate Transmission Ka Problem
Jab RBI Repo Rate kam karta hai taaki market me loans saste hon aur economic growth badhe, toh commercial banks apne lending interest rates kam karne me mahino laga dete the. Is issue ko solve karne ke liye RBI ne time-to-time 4 alag-alag lending frameworks introduce kiye:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 EVOLUTION OF BENCHMARK LENDING RATES                     |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2003: BPLR      -> Opaque, sub-PLR lending distorted retail rates        |
| 2010: Base Rate -> Average cost of funds, cannot lend below Base Rate    |
| 2016: MCLR      -> Marginal cost of funds, but transmission still slow   |
| 2019: EBLR      -> Linked to External Benchmarks (Repo/T-Bill), Instant! |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

### 2. Comprehensive Comparison of All 4 Lending Regimes — *[PDF Pages 144–146]*

| Parameter | BPLR (2003) | Base Rate (2010) | MCLR (2016) | EBLR (2019) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Full Form** | Benchmark Prime Lending Rate | Base Rate | Marginal Cost of Funds based Lending Rate | External Benchmark Lending Rate |
| **Effective Date**| 2003 | **1 July 2010** | **1 April 2016** | **1 October 2019** |
| **Committee** | RBI Working Group | **Deepak Mohanty Committee** | Internal Study Group | **Janak Raj Committee** |
| **Calculation Basis** | Bank's internal average cost of funds | Average Cost of Funds + Operating Costs + CRR carry + Return on Net Worth | **Marginal (Incremental)** Cost of Funds + Negative carry on CRR + Operating Costs + Tenor Premium | **External Market Benchmark** (Repo rate or T-Bill Yield) + Fixed Spread |
| **Sub-Benchmark Lending**| Openly allowed (Corporate loans were given below BPLR) | **Strictly Prohibited** (No loan below Base Rate except staff/DRI) | Strictly Prohibited | Strictly Prohibited |
| **Reset Frequency**| Discretionary | At least once every quarter | Set for specific tenors (Overnight, 1M, 3M, 6M, **1 Year**); reviewed monthly | **At least once every 3 Months (Quarterly)** mandatory! |
| **Transparency** | Very Poor & Opaque | Moderate | Better than Base Rate, but still internal | **100% Transparent & Objective** |

---

### 3. External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR) In-Depth — *[PDF Page 146]*
* **Mandatory w.e.f.:** **1 October 2019** for all new floating rate personal or retail loans (housing, auto) and floating rate loans to Micro and Small Enterprises (later extended to Medium Enterprises).
* **Eligible External Benchmarks (Banks must choose one):**
  1. **RBI Policy Repo Rate** *(Most popular; adopted by >80% banks)*.
  2. Government of India **91-day Treasury Bill yield** (FBIL benchmark).
  3. Government of India **182-day Treasury Bill yield** (FBIL benchmark).
  4. Any other benchmark market interest rate published by Financial Benchmark India Pvt Ltd (FBIL).
* **Reset Rule:** RBI repo rate change hone ke agle review cycle me, banks ko **at least once every 3 months** customer ka loan interest rate revise karna padega.

---

### 🧠 Quick Self-Check Quiz (Module 23)

   * *Answer:* 1 October 2019.
2. **Which committee recommended the introduction of the Base Rate system in 2010?**
   * *Answer:* Deepak Mohanty Committee.
3. **What are the four components used to determine MCLR?**
   * *Answer:* 1. Marginal cost of funds, 2. Negative carry on CRR, 3. Operating costs, 4. Tenor premium.
4. **How frequently must a bank reset its interest rate under the EBLR regime?**
   * *Answer:* At least once every three months.

## 💸 Module 24: Payment & Settlement Systems (NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, UPI, CTS)

### 1. National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT) — *[PDF Page 58]*
* **Introduced:** **November 2005**. Managed and operated by **RBI**.
* **Type:** Deferred Net Settlement (DNS) basis par batch-wise processing hoti hai.
* **Operating Hours:** **24x7x365** w.e.f. **16 December 2019**.
* **Batches:** Operates in **48 half-hourly batches** every day (starting from 00:30 hours to 00:00 hours).
* **Minimum & Maximum Limits:**
  * **Minimum Limit:** **No Limit (₹1 bhi transfer kar sakte hain)**.
  * **Maximum Limit:** **No ceiling limit** for regular online/branch NEFT.
  * Exception: Cash-based remittance to Nepal under Indo-Nepal Remittance Facility has a ceiling of ₹50,000 per transaction (walk-in customers).
* **Transaction Charges:** RBI ne **online NEFT transactions (via internet banking / mobile app) par savings account holders ke liye saare charges abolish kar diye hain** w.e.f. 1 Jan 2020!

---

### 2. Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) — *[PDF Page 58]*
* **Introduced:** 2004. Managed and operated directly by **RBI**.
* **Meaning of Terms:**
  * **Real Time:** Processing payment order aate hi bina kisi delay ke execute hoti hai.
  * **Gross Settlement:** Har ek transaction individual basis par settle hota hai (netting nahi hoti). Settlement RBI ke books me final aur irrevocable hota hai.
* **Operating Hours:** **24x7x365** w.e.f. **14 December 2020**.
* **Minimum Limit:** **₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)**! (This is an essential exam fact: Below ₹2 Lakh RTGS is NOT possible).
* **Maximum Limit:** **No Upper Limit**.

---

### 3. Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) — *[PDF Page 58]*
* **Introduced:** **22 November 2010**. Managed and operated by **NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India)**.
* **Platform:** Instant interbank electronic fund transfer 24x7x365 through mobile banking, internet banking, ATMs.
* **Maximum Transaction Limit:** **₹5 Lakh per transaction** (raised from ₹2 Lakh in October 2021).
* **Identifier:** 7-digit **MMID (Mobile Money Identifier)** + Mobile number, ya Account Number + IFSC.

---

### 4. NUUP (*99#) & Routing Codes — *[PDF Pages 59–60]*
* **NUUP (National Unified USSD Platform) [*99#]:**
  * Powered by NPCI. Unstructured Supplementary Service Data (USSD) technology par kaam karta hai.
  * **Bina kisi mobile internet ya smartphone ke** kisi bhi basic GSM feature phone se `*99#` dial karke interbank balance check, mini statement aur fund transfer kiya ja sakta hai!
* **MICR Code (Magnetic Ink Character Recognition) [PDF Page 60]:**
  * **9 Digits numeric code** printed on cheque bottom:
    * `Digits 1–3`: **City Code** (aligned with PIN code)
    * `Digits 4–6`: **Bank Code**
    * `Digits 7–9`: **Branch Code**
* **IFSC Code (Indian Financial System Code):**
  * **11 Characters alphanumeric code** used in NEFT, RTGS, IMPS:
    * `Characters 1–4`: **Bank Code** (alphabetic, e.g., `SBIN`)
    * `Character 5`: Reserved as **0 (Zero)** for future use
    * `Characters 6–11`: **Branch Code** (alphanumeric, e.g., `000123`)

---

### 🎯 Summary Comparison of Payment Systems
| Feature | NEFT | RTGS | IMPS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operator** | **RBI** | **RBI** | **NPCI** |
| **Settlement Type** | Half-hourly Batches (DNS) | Real-time Gross | Instant Individual |
| **Minimum Limit** | **₹1 (No floor)** | **₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh)** | **₹1 (No floor)** |
| **Maximum Limit** | **No Limit** | **No Limit** | **₹5,00,000 (₹5 Lakh)** |
| **Availability** | 24x7x365 | 24x7x365 | 24x7x365 |

## 🛑 Module 25: Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework for Banks & NBFCs

### 1. PCA Framework Kya Hai Aur Kyon Lagta Hai? — *[PDF Page 61]*
* **Introduced:** **2002** by RBI (revised substantially in April 2017 and November 2021).
* **Purpose:** Agar kisi bank ki financial health kharab hone lage (capital kam ho jaye, NPAs bohot badh jayein, ya leverage ratio gir jaye), toh uske band hone ka intezar kiye bina RBI timely **Supervisory Intervention** karta hai taaki bank ko dubne se bachaya ja sake.
* Yeh ek **Early Warning & Resolution Mechanism** hai.

---

### 2. Commercial Banks Ke 3 Risk Thresholds (Parameters) — *[PDF Pages 61–63]*

RBI banks ke 3 key indicators track karta hai:
1. **Capital:** CRAR (Capital to Risk Weighted Assets Ratio) and Tier 1 Capital Ratio.
2. **Asset Quality:** Net NPA Ratio (NNPA).
3. **Leverage:** Tier 1 Leverage Ratio.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                  PCA RISK THRESHOLDS (COMMERCIAL BANKS)                  |
+---------------------+---------------------+------------------------------+
| Risk Level          | Trigger Indicator   | Key Mandatory Actions        |
+---------------------+---------------------+------------------------------+
| **Risk Threshold 1**| CRAR < 9% (or NNPA  | Restriction on Dividend      |
|                     | $\ge$ 6.0%)         | distribution & profits;      |
|                     |                     | Promoters to bring capital   |
+---------------------+---------------------+------------------------------+
| **Risk Threshold 2**| CRAR < 9% (further  | In addition to Threshold 1:  |
|                     | drop) or NNPA       | **Restriction on Branch      |
|                     | $\ge$ 9.0%          | Expansion** (domestic & o/s) |
+---------------------+---------------------+------------------------------+
| **Risk Threshold 3**| Severe Capital drop | In addition to 1 & 2:        |
|                     | (CRAR < 6.0%)       | **Restrictions on Capital   |
|                     |                     | Expenditure & Director Pay** |
+---------------------+---------------------+------------------------------+
```

---

### 3. Mandatory vs Discretionary Actions under PCA — *[PDF Pages 62–63]*

#### 🔒 Mandatory Actions (Compulsory Actions):
* **Risk Threshold 1:**
  * Dividend distribution aur profits remittance par strict restriction.
  * Promoters/owners ko additional equity capital lane ka order.
* **Risk Threshold 2:**
  * Threshold 1 ki saari pabandiya + **Nayi branch kholne par complete ban** (domestic and overseas).
  * Higher provisions for NPAs.
* **Risk Threshold 3:**
  * Threshold 1 aur 2 ki saari pabandiya + **Capital expenditure par restrictions** (except for technological upgradation within approved limits).
  * Variable operating costs aur staff/director remuneration increase par ban.

#### 🛠️ Discretionary Actions (RBI's Discretion):
* **Supervisory / Governance Actions:** RBI board of directors ko supersede kar sakti hai, managerial staff ko remove kar sakti hai (under Section 36AA BR Act), aur bank ko merge/amalgamate karne ka process start kar sakti hai.
* **Credit Risk Actions [PDF Page 63]:** Fresh NPAs rokhne ke liye time-bound plan, loan concentration reduce karna, high-risk unsecured loans par restriction, aur recovery ke liye bad assets ki sale initiate karna.

---

### 4. PCA Framework for NBFCs — *[PDF Pages 64–68]*
* **Effective Date:** **1 October 2022** (based on financial position as on March 31, 2022).
* **Applicability:** All Deposit Taking NBFCs (**NBFCs-D**), Non-Deposit Taking NBFCs (**NBFCs-ND**) in Middle and Upper Layers, aur Core Investment Companies (**CICs**).
* **Indicators Tracked [PDF Page 64]:**
  * For NBFCs-D & NBFCs-ND: CRAR, Tier 1 Capital Ratio, Net NPA Ratio.
  * For CICs: Adjusted Net Worth / Aggregate RWA, Leverage Ratio, Net NPA.
* **Mandatory Actions on NBFCs [PDF Pages 65–68]:**
  * Dividend payout restriction, restriction on issuing guarantees, branch expansion curbs, reduction in variable operating costs, and mandatory recapitalization.

---

## 🏢 Module 26: Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) & Scale Based Regulation

### 1. NBFC Kya Hai Aur Bank Se Kaise Alag Hai? — *[PDF Pages 157–158]*
* **Definition:** Ek aisi company jo **Companies Act, 1956 / 2013** ke tehat registered hoti hai aur jiska mukhya business loans dena, shares/bonds/securities acquire karna, leasing, hire-purchase, ya chit business karna hota hai.
* **Registration:** **Section 45-IA of RBI Act, 1934** ke tehat RBI se Certificate of Registration (CoR) lena mandatory hota hai.
* **Exempted NBFCs (to avoid dual regulation) [PDF Page 158]:** Stock broking / Merchant banking companies (regulated by SEBI), Housing Finance Companies (regulated by NHB/RBI), Insurance companies (regulated by IRDAI), Nidhi companies & Chit funds (regulated by Ministry of Corporate Affairs).

#### ⚖️ Banks vs NBFCs: Key Differences Table (Exam Must-Know!):
| Feature | Commercial Banks | NBFCs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Acceptance of Demand Deposits**| **YES** (Savings & Current a/c) | **STRICTLY NO!** (Cannot accept demand deposits) |
| **Payment & Settlement System** | **YES** (Integral part; RTGS, NEFT, clearing) | **NO** (Not part of payment & settlement system) |
| **Issuing Cheques on Themselves**| **YES** (Can issue cheques) | **STRICTLY NO!** (Cannot draw cheques on itself) |
| **Deposit Insurance (DICGC Cover)**| **YES! Up to ₹5 Lakh per depositor** | **NO! Zero DICGC insurance cover** |
| **CRR Requirement** | Mandatory under Section 42 | No CRR requirement (only SLR for deposit NBFCs) |

---

### 2. 50-50 Principal Business Test & Net Owned Funds (NOF) — *[PDF Pages 158, 160]*

#### 🧪 50-50 Principal Business Criteria:
Koi company NBFC tabhi maani jaati hai jab woh dono conditions puri kare:
1. Uske **Total Assets ka kam se kam 50% hissa Financial Assets** ho.
2. Uski **Gross Income ka kam se kam 50% hissa Financial Assets se derived** ho.

#### 💰 Minimum Net Owned Funds (NOF) Requirement:
* Historical requirement: ₹2 Crore (since April 1999).
* **Revised NOF Framework (Effective 1 October 2022) [PDF Page 160]:**
  * RBI has mandated **₹10 Crore as Net Owned Funds** for NBFC-ICC (Investment and Credit Company), NBFC-MFI, and NBFC-Factors (phased timeline to achieve ₹10 Cr).
  * For NBFC-P2P (Peer to Peer Lending), NBFC-AA (Account Aggregator), NOF is ₹2 Crore.
  * For Mortgage Guarantee Companies (MGC): **₹100 Crore** minimum NOF required at commencement!

---

### 3. Scale Based Regulation (SBR): The 4-Tier Architecture — *[PDF Page 163]*
RBI ne **October 2022** se NBFCs ke regulation ko unki size, activity aur systemic risk ke basis par 4 layers me divide kiya hai:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                 SCALE BASED REGULATION (SBR) 4 LAYERS                    |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  TOP LAYER (NBFC-TL)      | Empty by default; populated only if extreme  |
|                           | systemic risk arises in an Upper Layer NBFC  |
+---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+
|  UPPER LAYER (NBFC-UL)    | Top 50 identified systemically important     |
|                           | NBFCs; Bank-like strict regulations apply    |
+---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+
|  MIDDLE LAYER (NBFC-ML)   | All Deposit NBFCs (NBFC-D) + Non-deposit     |
|                           | NBFCs with assets >= ₹1,000 Crore + HFCs     |
+---------------------------+----------------------------------------------+
|  BASE LAYER (NBFC-BL)     | Non-deposit NBFCs with assets < ₹1,000 Crore |
|                           | + NBFC-P2P + NBFC-AA + NOFHC                 |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

1. **Base Layer (NBFC-BL):** Non-deposit taking NBFCs below asset size of ₹1,000 Crore; NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, and Type-I NBFC-ND.
2. **Middle Layer (NBFC-ML):** All Deposit-taking NBFCs (irrespective of asset size); Non-deposit taking NBFCs with asset size of **₹1,000 Crore and above**; Standalone Primary Dealers (SPDs), Infrastructure Debt Funds (IDFs), Core Investment Companies (CICs), and Housing Finance Companies (HFCs).
3. **Upper Layer (NBFC-UL):** RBI dwara specific scoring methodology ke basis par top identified NBFCs. **Top 10 eligible NBFCs by asset size will always reside in the Upper Layer!** Bank-like capital adequacy and CET1 ratio apply.
4. **Top Layer (NBFC-TL):** Normally empty rehti hai; agar Upper Layer ki koi NBFC extreme systemic contagion risk ban jaye, toh RBI use Top Layer me daal kar higher capital surcharge impose karegi.

### 4. Specialized NBFC Categories — *[PDF Pages 159, 161–162]*
* **Core Investment Company (CIC-ND-SI) [PDF Page 161]:**
  * Asset size $\ge$ **₹100 Crore**.
  * Holds at least **90% of total assets** in equity, preference shares, bonds or loans in group companies.
  * Investments in **equity shares of group companies must be at least 60%** of total assets!
* **NBFC-Factor [PDF Page 162]:**
  * Principal business: Financial assets in factoring business must constitute at least **50% of total assets** (earlier 75%, amended by Factoring Regulation Amendment Act 2021), and income from factoring must be at least **50% of gross income**. Net Owned Funds: **₹5 Crore**.
* **Mortgage Guarantee Company (MGC) [PDF Page 159]:**
  * Notified under Section 45-I(f)(iii) of RBI Act. Housing loan defaults ke against lenders ko guarantee provide karti hai. Minimum NOF: **₹100 Crore**.
* **NBFC-P2P (Peer-to-Peer):** Online platform jo individual lenders aur borrowers ko connect karta hai.
* **NBFC-AA (Account Aggregator):** User ki consent se financial data ko banks/institutions ke beech digitally share karne ka platform provide karta hai (cannot access or store user data).

### 🧠 Quick Self-Check Quiz (Module 26)
1. **Can an NBFC accept demand deposits from the public?**
   * *Answer:* Strictly NO! NBFCs can only accept term deposits (if licensed as NBFC-D).
2. **What is the minimum Net Owned Funds (NOF) requirement for an NBFC-ICC effective from October 2022?**
   * *Answer:* ₹10 Crore.
3. **Under Scale Based Regulation (SBR), how many eligible NBFCs by asset size will always reside in the Upper Layer?**
   * *Answer:* Top 10 eligible NBFCs.
4. **What percentage of total assets must a Core Investment Company (CIC) hold in group companies?**
   * *Answer:* At least 90% (with at least 60% in equity shares of group companies).
